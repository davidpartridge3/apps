🔥 Finisher · 10–15 min

Choose ONE: incline treadmill walk, stationary bike, or sled. Example: 40 s moderate → 20 s push, repeat for 10–12 min. Don't turn this into an all-out HIIT session every week — the goal is extra cardiovascular work without compromising recovery.

⏱️ Rest period rules
Heavy compound
Hack squat · RDL · DB press · rows · shoulder press		2–3 min
Moderate compound
Lunges · pulldowns · chest press · DB bench		90–120 s
Isolation
Laterals · curls · triceps · leg extensions · leg curls · calves		45–90 s
Rule: if you're still significantly out of breath or your muscles haven't recovered enough to perform the next set properly, take additional rest. Don't sacrifice performance just to hit an arbitrary rest timer.
📈 Double progression
For a rep range like 3 × 6–10: start with a weight you can perform for roughly 10 / 9 / 8. Over subsequent workouts, increase total reps → 10 / 10 / 9 → 10 / 10 / 10. Once you hit the top of the rep range on every set with good form, increase the weight and repeat the process. 70-lb DB Incline Press Week 1 → 10 / 9 / 8 Week 2 → 10 / 10 / 9 Week 3 → 10 / 10 / 10 ⬆ increase to 75 lb Week 4 → 8 / 7 / 6 … continue progressing
🎯 RIR — reps in reserve
Compound movements: generally 1–3 RIR
Isolation movements: generally 0–2 RIR
Final isolation set: occasionally 0–1 RIR

You do NOT need to take every set to failure. The goal is high-quality hard sets while maintaining enough recovery to progress week after week.
🧠 Training principles
1. Progressive overload — gradually increase weight OR reps OR quality of execution over time.

2. Mechanical tension > chasing the pump — the pump is enjoyable, but prioritise load + ROM + proximity to failure + progressive overload.

3. Controlled eccentrics — roughly 2–3 s down. Don't obsess over counting seconds; the goal is controlled → stable → full ROM rather than dropping the weight.

4. Full ROM — meaningful stretch and complete contraction. Especially incline curls, RDLs, leg curls, leg extensions, calf raises, cable flyes and lateral raises.

5. Partial reps — an optional intensity technique, not something that needs to happen every workout. Best used occasionally at the end of isolation work when already near failure.
🥩 Recovery targets
Protein: ~0.7–1.0 g per lb bodyweight per day, spread across several meals.
Sleep: 7–9 hours per night.
Hydration: consistent throughout the day.
Calories: small surplus for muscle gain · around maintenance + high protein for recomposition. Avoid aggressive bulking if minimising fat gain is important.

Cardio: 2–3 sessions per week, mostly 20–30 min moderate intensity. Saturday's post-workout finisher counts as one.
📊 Progress tracking
Record after every workout: exercise → weight → reps → RIR. Incline DB Press 70s — 10 / 9 / 8 ~2 RIR next time → 70s — 10 / 10 / 9 Track weekly: body weight, key lifts, reps/weights, waist measurement, progress photos, general energy and recovery.

Evaluate after 6–8 weeks. If strength/reps are progressing and measurements are moving in the desired direction — keep the program. Don't change exercises simply because you're getting bored.
⭐ Primary goal
Train hard enough to stimulate growth. Recover enough to grow. Progress consistently.

The program does not need to destroy you. Consistency + progressive overload over months = results.
